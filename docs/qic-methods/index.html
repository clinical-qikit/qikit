    qic(): methods and conformance¶

    This document records how qic() computes center lines, limits and signals, which published source each choice follows, and every place it departs from that source. It is the time-series counterpart of methods.md, which covers funnels.

    qic() follows R's qicharts2 [1] unless stated otherwise. tests/test_reference.py compares it with qicharts2, and with NHSRplotthedots [5] for method="nhs", on the packages' own data to 1e-6. The R output lives in fixtures/reference/ and is regenerated by scripts/make_reference_fixtures.R. Each accepted difference is listed in the test's DEPARTURES table and in §4 below.

    1. The pipeline¶

    src/qikit/spc.py runs these steps in order:

    1. Normalize. Columns or sequences become one frame. n= divides y on every chart, as in qicharts2, so an i chart of a rate is chart="i", y=events, n=cases.
    2. Aggregate. Rows that share an x become one subgroup: numerators and denominators summed, raw values combined by agg_fun=. Numeric and date x are sorted first, so runs and moving ranges follow time, not row order. Text x keeps its order. x_period= buckets dates.
    3. Baseline. freeze=k sets the limits from points 1..k. part= splits the chart into phases; part=k and freeze=k both mean "ends at point k". exclude= drops points from the limits and the rules but still draws them.
    4. Limits. core/shewhart.py, per part, from that part's baseline points.
    5. Rules. core/rules.py, per part, by method=.
    6. Table. result.data and result.summary.

    2. Center lines and limits¶

    σ̂ is estimated from the baseline points of each part.

    Chart Center line σ̂ Source
    run median none [1]
    i mean screened MR̄/d₂ (MR above 3.267·MR̄ dropped, then MR̄ recomputed) [1, 2]
    mr MR̄ UCL = D₄·MR̄ = 3.267·MR̄, LCL = 0 [2]
    xbar grand mean, weighted by subgroup size Sp/c₄(d + 1), pooled over baseline subgroups [2]
    s c₄(nᵢ)·σ̂ as xbar [2]
    t i chart on y^(1/3.6), back-transformed as i [1, 3]
    p Σy/Σn √(p̄(1 − p̄)/nᵢ); UCL capped at 1, LCL floored at 0 [2]
    pp Σy/Σn Laney: σ_z·√(p̄(1 − p̄)/nᵢ) [4]
    c mean √c̄ [2]
    u Σy/Σn √(ū/nᵢ) [2]
    up Σy/Σn Laney: σ_z·√(ū/nᵢ) [4]
    g median, drawn as CL limits from the mean ḡ: ḡ ± 3√(ḡ(ḡ + 1)), LCL floored at 0 [1, 2 p. 319]

    c₄ is exact, √(2/(n − 1))·Γ(n/2)/Γ((n − 1)/2), not a table. A₃, B₃ and B₄ are derived from it. The TypeScript engine computes the same function.

    Laney's σ_z is the screened MR̄ of the standardized values zᵢ divided by 1.128, with no floor at 1, as in qicharts2. Under-dispersed data therefore draw limits narrower than the p or u chart. A series with every z equal has σ_z = 0 and NaN limits.

    The 2σ lines (ucl_95, lcl_95) are CL ± 2σ̂ on every chart, capped like the 3σ lines. They are what the 2-of-3 rules read.

    method="nhs" on an i chart uses Making Data Count's 2.66·MR̄ for the 3σ half-width, with the same screened MR̄ [5]. 3/d₂ = 2.6596, so the difference is in the fourth significant figure.

    3. Rules (method=)¶

    A point beyond the 3σ limits is the chart's own test: signal = "outlier". The rules find patterns and mark the points that form them in runs_signal.

    3.1 "anhoej" (default)¶

    Anhøj and Olesen's rules [6], as qicharts2 implements them. Points on the center line are not useful and are skipped.

    • Longest run. A signal when the longest run on one side is longer than round(log₂ n) + 3, where n is the number of useful points.
    • Crossings. A signal when the number of crossings is fewer than the 5th percentile of Binomial(n − 1, ½), qbinom(0.05, n − 1, 0.5).
    • No minimum n. qicharts2 applies both rules at any n.

    summary reports n_useful, longest_run, longest_run_max, n_crossings, n_crossings_min, longest_run_signal and crossings_signal. Too few crossings is a property of the whole series, so the plot can color every point (runs_highlight=).

    Sensitivity. Before 1.0 qikit used floor(log₂ n) + 3 with "at least", which flags runs one or two points shorter. Simulated with the median from the same data, false signals from the run rule fall from 2–10% to 0–2% across n = 12 to 48. Power to see a 1 SD shift in the last half falls from 18% to 8% at n = 20 and from 45% to 19% at n = 48. The crossings rule still catches most sustained shifts. "nhs" or "ihi" recover the shorter run under a published rule set.

    3.2 "nhs": Making Data Count¶

    NHS England's rules for XmR charts [5, 7], as NHSRplotthedots implements them:

    Rule Condition
    Beyond limits a point outside the 3σ limits
    7-point trend 7 points in a row strictly rising or falling; a repeated value breaks it
    2 of 3 near a limit 2 of 3 consecutive points beyond 2σ, all three on the same side
    7 on one side 7 points in a row on one side of the mean; a point on the mean breaks it

    Each point takes the first of these it belongs to, in the order of the table, as its special_cause. A point inside a trend takes the trend's direction; at the join of two trends, the one it starts.

    With worse=, each point's variation is "improving", "concerning" or "common". Without it, a special cause is "neutral". summary["variation"] is the last point's, which is what the Making Data Count icon shows.

    With target= and worse=, summary["assurance"] is judged at the last point's limits. It is "pass" when the target lies beyond the limit on the bad side, so the process cannot miss it by chance. It is "fail" when the target lies beyond the limit on the good side, and "variable" when it lies inside the limits.

    3.3 "ihi": Provost and Murray¶

    From The Health Care Data Guide [3]. Repeated values are passed over in a trend.

    • Run chart: a shift of 6 or more on one side of the median, and a trend of 5 or more.
    • Shewhart chart: a shift of 8, a trend of 6, 2 of 3 beyond 2σ on one side, and 15 in a row within 1σ.

    The run chart's runs-count table is not implemented. Anhøj's crossings rule, the default method, answers the same question with a formula rather than a table.

    3.4 "weco" and "nelson"¶

    The Western Electric zone rules [8] and Nelson's eight tests [9], less the point beyond 3σ, which is signal. Both need limits, so they do not apply to a run chart.

    3.5 Short series: provisional and the notes¶

    A chart of a few points cannot show that a process is stable, so qikit says so in words. It never changes signal, runs_signal or the limits.

    Baseline points Charts What qikit does
    fewer than 12 all summary["provisional"] is True, a note says the series is too short to judge, and print(result) reads "No signal so far" in place of "looks stable"
    12 to 19 those with control limits (not "run") a note calls the limits provisional; provisional stays False
    20 or more all nothing

    The count is of the points that set the current center line: the baseline points of the last part. freeze=10 therefore leaves a chart provisional however many points follow, and so does a new part (part=) that is still short.

    Where the numbers come from. They are rules of thumb, set as the constants MIN_POINTS and MIN_LIMIT_POINTS in spc.py, and published guidance does not agree on one figure:

    • 20 is the usual figure for control limits from a stable baseline (Provost and Murray [3]; Montgomery [2] asks for 20 to 25 subgroups).
    • 12 is qikit's own choice. It lies between the roughly 10 points at which run-chart guidance sets a baseline median (Perla, Provost and Murray [10]) and the 15 that Making Data Count [7] asks for before drawing process limits. A team reporting under Making Data Count should read 12 to 14 points as too few for limits, which the 12-to-19 note already says.

    The run rules give the practical reason for caution (§3.1): at n = 20 they have 8% power to see a 1 SD shift in the last half of the series, so no signal on a short series says little.

    4. Departures from qicharts2¶

    Each is deliberate and listed in tests/test_reference.py.

    • The MR chart's 2σ line. qikit draws MR̄(1 + 2·d₃/d₂) = 2.511·MR̄, the moving range's own 2σ. qicharts2 draws two-thirds of the UCL, 2.178·MR̄. Only ucl_95 is affected.
    • xbar with unequal subgroups. qikit uses ±3σ̂/√nᵢ with the unbiased σ̂ = Sp/c₄(d + 1), d the pooled degrees of freedom. qicharts2 uses A₃(nᵢ)·Sp, which corrects with c₄(nᵢ). The two differ by a fraction of a percent.
    • s with unequal subgroups. qikit's center line is c₄(nᵢ)·σ̂, the expected sᵢ, so it moves with nᵢ. qicharts2 draws a flat CL = Sp with B₃/B₄(nᵢ) limits. With equal subgroups the two agree.
    • A moving range across a part boundary is NaN in qikit, so one part's spread never leaks into the next. qicharts2 computes each part separately and agrees.

    5. What changed in 1.0¶

    Each change below moves results on some data. The CHANGELOG has the full list.

    Change Effect on signals
    Run rule: round(log₂ n) + 3 with "longer than" Fewer false signals, less power (§3.1)
    xbar/s spread from the baseline and each part More sensitive after a real change
    g chart limits from the mean Limits about 10–15% wider
    Laney σ_z: screened, no floor Narrower limits on under-dispersed series
    ihi run-chart rules 6/5; zone rules on Shewhart charts More sensitive, as published
    n= divides y on every chart; p UCL capped at 1; MR LCL 0 None on valid data
