    Across providers: funnel()¶

    Each provider is plotted at its own volume, against Spiegelhalter's exact 99.8% and 95% probability contours (Statistics in Medicine 2005;24:1185-1202). Every method, reference and design choice is recorded in Methods: funnel plots and over-dispersion, including where qikit departs from the paper.

    Choosing the settings¶

    You have Pass

    Rows that share a unit are summed, so stay-level or year-level data aggregates itself.

    Reading the result¶

    r.data has one row per provider:

    Column Meaning
    unit, label The provider and its display name
    y The plotted value: O/E, proportion or rate
    numerator, denominator The summed y= and n= (observed and expected, events and cases)
    cl The center line: pooled ΣO/ΣE (or Σevents/Σn) unless center= is given
    lower_outlier, upper_outlier The 99.8% contour at this provider's volume
    lower_alert, upper_alert The 95% contour
    p_value Exact p-value against the model the contours are drawn from; one-sided toward worse=, doubled when two-sided
    q_value Its Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across the chart's providers
    signal "outlier", "alert" or "none". The tiers never overlap
    direction "higher" or "lower" than the center line
    min_detectable The smallest true value this provider could have flagged with 80% power
    ci_lower, ci_upper Exact 95% interval for the provider's own value
    excluded Left out with exclude= (drawn faded, not tested)
    raw_excess (observed − cl × denominator), signed so positive is worse: excess days or events
    reliability How much of the provider's difference is signal, 0–1. 0 everywhere when there is no measurable spread
    eb_rate, eb_excess The shrunk (empirical Bayes) value, the exact posterior mean, and its excess over the reference. Rank flagged providers on eb_excess
    eb_low, eb_high A 95% (interval=) interval for the provider's true value from the same posterior; NaN with no measurable spread
    prob_worse Posterior probability that the true value is worse than the reference (CMS-style "worse than average"); NaN with no spread
    estimate_flag "worse", "better" or "no different": where the interval lies against the reference ("higher"/"lower" without worse=)
    p_value_drop1 The p-value with the provider's single most extreme case removed (needs case rows or max_case_y=/max_case_n=); descriptive, no q-value
    flag_survives_drop1 For flagged providers: still flagged without that case?
    fragility Case rows: the fewest worst cases whose removal clears the flag (1 = one case carries it)
    effective_denominator Only with a within_dispersion column: n/φᵢ, the plot's x axis
    benchmark, meets_benchmark Only with benchmark=: the goal (same on every row of a facet), and whether this provider is at it or better
    benchmark_group Only with benchmark="abc": whether this provider is one of the best performers the ABC pools

    r.summary holds the model and how to read the counts:

    • n_outlier, n_alert, and expected_false_flags.
    • tau: the estimated spread of true values between providers; tau_method, tau_scale, tau_source ("estimated" or "pre-specified"), tau_estimated, and tau_ci under tau_method="ml".
    • phi: the over-dispersion ratio as the chosen method defines it.
    • reference: what the estimate columns are judged against (the center line, or 1.0 under compare_to="model"), and estimate_tau, the spread of their prior.
    • count_model, limit_method, within_dispersion, dispersion_shape, underpowered, n_filtered, n_fragile.
    • With benchmark=: benchmark, a dict of method, share and value. Under "abc" it also holds:
    • providers and group_denominator: who sets it and their volumes.
    • coverage: their share of patients.
    • msd and n_below_msd: Kiefe's minimum sufficient denominator.
    • without_small: the ABC without providers below it.
    • notes: sentences ready to show a reader.

    On a faceted funnel the same keys are under by_facet.

    What decides a flag¶

    Each contour is the boundary of an exact test. A provider is beyond the 99.8% line exactly when its one-sided p-value is below 0.001, and beyond the 95% line exactly when it is below 0.025. For the unadjusted O/E chart that is the same as "the provider's own exact 95% interval excludes the center line". The line, the p-value, the color and the interval always agree.

    signal= picks one test for both tiers:

    • "limits" (default): outlier beyond the outer contour, alert beyond the inner one. About 0.1% of average providers land beyond each 99.8% line by chance, and 2.5% beyond each 95% line. summary["expected_false_flags"] counts that for your chart. With 1,000 providers on a one-sided chart it is about 1 outlier and 24 alerts, so "35 alerts" is read against that. A point beyond the 95% line can therefore have a large q_value. For an action list use signal="fdr" or q_value; to keep signal="limits" but draw the points by their q tiers, use .plot(color_by="fdr") (below).
    • "fdr": outlier when q_value < 0.05, alert when q_value < 0.10 (fdr_levels=). At most that share of each tier is expected to be false, however many providers there are. The flags then no longer match the drawn lines, and the caption says so.

    There is deliberately no "limit OR q" mode. That union only ever adds flags, so it never removes the chance alerts that make FDR worth using, and neither guarantee holds for the combined tier (methods §4.11).

    limits=(0.998, 0.95) moves the contours, for example to (0.997, 0.95).

    Over-dispersion: real differences between providers¶

    No risk model captures everything, so providers differ by more than chance allows. With overdispersion=True (the default) each provider is judged against that spread as well as chance, using Spiegelhalter's additive random-effects model. The smallest providers' limits barely move. The busiest level off at about the center ± z·τ instead of closing onto the line. When τ̂ is 0, which is common at physician scale, the chart is identical to overdispersion=False. That holds around the pooled center only. Around a fixed center=, τ̂ also absorbs any system-wide gap between the providers and that center, so it can be large. Every limit then widens, and qikit raises a UserWarning saying by how much (methods §4.2.1). For "worse than the risk model?" pass compare_to="model", which turns the allowance off.

    How τ is estimated (tau_method=, methods §4.16):

    tau_method= What it is Use when
    "spiegelhalter" (default) 10%-winsorized method of moments, as published Screening. The most sensitive robust option, and uncorrected like the NHS and CQC estimators
    "winsorized" The same with the consistency factor of his eq. 9 Flags acted on one by one, where the 0.1% and 2.5% chance rates must hold
    "ml" Maximum likelihood, with an interval tau_ci Data already cleaned of outliers; not robust
    "cqc", "shmi" CQC (√ or arcsine scale) and NHS SHMI (log scale), as FunnelPlotR computes them Reproducing those indicators (methods §4.17)
    tau=<value> Fixed in advance from earlier periods Fewer than 30 providers, or a stable benchmark

    The published estimator understates a small real spread, so average providers flag somewhat more often than the stated rate. That is the right error for a screen whose flags are reviewed. funnel_sensitivity() runs each option and marks the providers whose tier depends on the choice:

    from qikit import funnel_sensitivity

s = funnel_sensitivity(df, y="deaths", n="expected", measure="oe", unit="surgeon")
s[~s.consistent]      # flagged under one model and not another: a judgment call

    Treat a non-zero τ̂ as a finding about the system: unmeasured case mix, data differences, or real variation in practice.

    Minimum denominators¶

    A minimum denominator is the volume at which a provider's score is mostly signal. reliability rises with the denominator, so each reliability target has one floor, and min_denominator() returns it with its range and the providers on each side of it:

    r = funnel(df, y="deaths", n="expected", measure="oe", unit="surgeon")
floors = r.min_denominator([0.5, 0.7, 0.9])
floors[["reliability", "min_denominator", "min_low", "min_high", "n_above", "n_below"]]
floors.attrs["notes"]         # cautions to show with it

    The floor is in the units of n=: expected events for O/E, cases for proportions and means, exposure for rates. 0.7 is the usual target for high-stakes use; the target is a decision about the use, not a statistical result. When the data show no spread between providers there is no floor, and the result says so instead of inventing one.

    To decide where to roll small providers up to, compare the levels of the hierarchy. Each level is fitted separately, because the spread between groups is not the spread between physicians:

    from qikit import funnel_rollup

funnel_rollup(df, units=["surgeon", "practice", "hospital"], reliability=0.7,
              y="deaths", n="expected", measure="oe")

    Methods §4.14 has the formula.

    Length of stay and other clustered counts¶

    A funnel of observed/expected patient-days treats every day as an independent event. Days come in stays, so a provider's day total varies 5–10 times more than that assumption allows. Every small provider's limits come out far too narrow, and every p-value and q-value too small. τ cannot absorb this, because the missing variance shrinks with volume while τ does not.

    Pass stay-level rows and let qikit measure it:

    stays = session.sql("""
    SELECT provider_id, provider_name, los_days, expected_los_days
    FROM inpatient_stays WHERE discharge_date >= '2026-01-01'
""").to_pandas()

r = funnel(stays, y="LOS_DAYS", n="EXPECTED_LOS_DAYS", measure="oe",
           unit="PROVIDER_ID", label="PROVIDER_NAME",
           within_dispersion="estimate", worse="higher")
r.summary["within_dispersion"]     # φ̂: how much more variable each count is than Poisson

    Decide which question the chart answers before looking, and say it in the call:

    args = dict(y="LOS_DAYS", n="EXPECTED_LOS_DAYS", measure="oe", unit="PROVIDER_ID",
            within_dispersion="estimate", worse="higher")

# Is this provider unusual among its peers? Pooled ΣO/ΣE center, spread allowed.
peers = funnel(stays, **args, compare_to="peers")

# Do this provider's stays run longer than the risk model (Vizient, CMS) expects?
model = funnel(stays, **args, compare_to="model")

    If the whole system runs long against the model, model flags many providers and peers few: that gap is a finding about the system, not about one provider. Passing center=1.0 alone answers neither question (see the over-dispersion section above).

    • The estimate: φ̂ comes from each stay's residual around its own provider's rate, so real differences between providers stay with τ.
    • Already-aggregated data: pass a number, computed once in SQL: SUM(POWER(los - exp_los, 2) / exp_los) / (COUNT(*) - COUNT(DISTINCT provider)).
    • Event counts (deaths, readmissions) need nothing; leave the default of 1.
    • The guard: if the spread looks clustered and within_dispersion was left at 1, qikit raises a UserWarning and says so in summary["notes"] and on the figure.
    • Upstream choices: cap extreme stays at a stated percentile before aggregating. If φ̂ differs a lot between service lines, facet by service line; "estimate" is then taken per facet.
    • Multi-item surveys are the same problem on a proportion: one patient answers the three doctor questions alike, so item counts look like more data than they are. Pass one row per survey with within_dispersion="estimate"; the model then sees y/φ̂ out of n/φ̂ (Rao–Scott effective counts), interpolated rather than rounded (methods §4.13).

    In one simulated length-of-stay cohort of 1,000 providers with no real differences between them, the chart flags 24 outliers and 65 alerts without the fix. With within_dispersion="estimate" it flags 3 and 20, against 1 and 24 expected by chance (methods §5 has the full simulation).

    Physician-level comparison: what the chart could not find¶

    At individual-physician volumes a funnel is mostly a test with no power. The chance of flagging a physician whose true O/E is 2.0 at the 99.8% limit:

    Expected deaths 2 5 10 20 50
    Chance of flagging a true O/E of 2.0 5% 21% 44% 81% 99.8%

    Most physicians sit at 5–20 expected events, so absence of a signal is not evidence of acceptable performance. That is exactly how a blank funnel tends to get read. So every provider carries min_detectable and an exact interval. summary["underpowered"] says whether the typical provider could detect even a doubling, and an underpowered figure captions itself so the caveat survives a slide deck. A physician at O/E 2.5 whose min_detectable is 4.6 has not been cleared; the chart cannot see that far at their volume.

    The workflow that works is to aggregate until the chart has power. With the bundled example (24 surgeons, 3 years):

    import pandas as pd
from qikit import funnel

df = pd.read_csv("data/examples/physician_mortality_oe.csv")

# Any single year: no outlier, and underpowered is True.
funnel(df[df.year == 2025], y="observed_deaths", n="expected_deaths",
       measure="oe", unit="physician").summary["underpowered"]       # True

# All three years (rows per physician are summed): three real signals, SURG-07 and
# SURG-15 above, and a high performer, SURG-21, below the lower limit.
r = funnel(df, y="observed_deaths", n="expected_deaths", measure="oe", unit="physician")
r.data[r.data.signal != "none"]

    Physician scores are less reliable than they look. Hofer et al. found that physicians accounted for a small share of the variation in diabetes care, that reliability at typical panel sizes was low, and that a physician could improve a report card by dropping a few of their sickest patients (JAMA 1999). The fragility and p_value_drop1 columns exist for that last point. For hospitalists, Goodwin et al. found meaningful variation in length of stay and discharge destination but very little in 30-day readmission or mortality, and much of the hospitalist variation was explained by the hospital (J Gen Intern Med 2013). Expect low reliability for physician readmissions, and facet by hospital or campus (facets=) before comparing physicians.

    Estimates with a range¶

    Every funnel also gives each provider a shrunk estimate with an interval, the output a CMS-style hierarchical model reports (Keenan et al., Circ Cardiovasc Qual Outcomes 2008), without writing one. A provider's true value has an exact posterior under the funnel's own model, and the interval is from that posterior:

    from qikit import funnel

r = funnel(stays, y="READMIT", n="VIZIENT_EXPECTED", measure="oe",
           unit="ATTENDING_NPI", label="ATTENDING_NAME",
           compare_to="model", worse="higher")
r.data[["label", "y", "eb_rate", "eb_low", "eb_high", "prob_worse", "estimate_flag"]]
r.plot(kind="caterpillar")
    • What the interval means. eb_low–eb_high is a 95% (interval=) range for the provider's true value. Low-volume providers are pulled most toward the center line (under compare_to="model", toward the system's pooled value). Shrunk estimates are steadier and forecast next year's performance better than raw ones, especially at low volume (Dimick et al., Health Serv Res 2010). The pull also means a small provider's interval usually crosses the reference. That is "not enough data", not "fine".
    • estimate_flag and signal answer different questions. signal asks whether a provider is unusual given chance and the spread between providers. estimate_flag asks whether its true value is clearly above or below the reference (1.0 against the risk model, else the center line). Neither replaces the other, and estimate_flag has no multiplicity control: across many average providers about 5% of intervals miss.
    • Against the risk model, the limits and signal stay exactly those of compare_to="model" (1.0, no spread). The estimates shrink toward the pooled value, so a whole system running long shows every provider above 1.0, with the worst leading.
    • No measurable spread, and the interval columns are empty with a note. Pass tau= from earlier periods, or compare within a larger peer group.
    • The caterpillar plot sorts providers by eb_rate, draws each interval as a bar colored by estimate_flag, the raw value as a faint circle, and the reference as a dotted line. Ranks are very uncertain even when real differences exist (Marshall and Spiegelhalter, BMJ 1998), so read the intervals, not the order. With hundreds of providers, top= and bottom= keep the ends, and only flagged providers are named.

    The spread of the prior is estimated by maximum likelihood whatever tau_method the limits use: the published estimator understates it and would leave 95% intervals covering as few as half of true values (methods §4.14, §5).

    For your own risk model or covariates, the notebook docs/examples/hierarchical_readmissions.ipynb fits the full patient-level hierarchical model in PyMC (installed separately), with an optional hospital level, next to the funnel() equivalent, and compares the two.

    An achievable goal: benchmark=¶

    The center line is the average, and the average is not a goal. benchmark= adds one the best providers in the same data already reach:

    • "abc", the achievable benchmark of care (Kiefe et al. 1998), for process measures. It is the pooled rate of the best providers who together hold at least 10% of patients. Providers are ranked by (x + 1)/(n + 2) so that 3 of 3 cannot top the list on luck.
    • "eb", for outcomes. It is the 90th percentile, toward better, of the shrunk estimates eb_rate (O'Brien et al. 2008).
    r = funnel(df, y="hp_within_24h", n="admissions", measure="proportion", unit="physician",
           worse="lower", benchmark="abc", multiply=100)
b = r.summary["benchmark"]
b["value"]                           # e.g. 97.0: the % the best physicians achieve
r.data[r.data.benchmark_group]       # who set it
b["without_small"]                   # does it lean on low-volume physicians?
r.plot()                             # the benchmark as a dashed line

funnel(df, y="deaths", n="expected", measure="oe", unit="surgeon", worse="higher",
       benchmark="eb").summary["benchmark"]["value"]   # an achievable O/E

    The ABC's authors designed it for process measures. They urge extreme caution with outcomes, and discourage its use for pay, credentialing or public report cards (methods §4.18). Both are goals for confidential feedback, not tests, so signal is unchanged.

    Figures¶

    HOUSE = {"font": {"family": "Segoe UI", "size": 13}, "limits": {"color": "#555"}}

r.plot(
    styles=[HOUSE, {"lcl": {"visible": False}, "lcl_95": {"visible": False}}],
    x_range=(2, None),          # start at 2 expected deaths; the y axis refits
    label_signals="outlier",
    width=1000, height=560,
)

r.plot(color_by="fdr", fdr_watch=0.20)   # points by q tier; the lines stay the 95%/99.8% limits
    • Defaults for a projector: contours at 3.5:1 contrast, the 95% line dashed, points at 0.85 opacity, and the legend on. Outliers are red circles and alerts amber diamonds, so the tier never depends on color alone.
    • Axes and caption: the y axis is fitted to the providers and the narrow end of the funnel, not its low-volume flare. An unset caption states the flag rule.
    • Color by q: r.plot(color_by="fdr") colors points by their q tiers (fdr_levels) on a signal="limits" result, so the figure matches a q_value action list while r.data["signal"] and the drawn lines stay as they are. fdr_watch=0.20 adds a pale open-diamond watch tier for q < 0.20, on the figure only.
    • styles= restyles by role: data, outlier, alert, watch, runs, improving, concerning, neutral, cl, ucl, lcl, ucl_95, lcl_95, target, the groups limits and limits_95, end_labels and font. A list of dicts is merged in order, so a house style is one constant.
    • r.limits(n=None) returns the contours at any denominators, for drawing elsewhere.
    • r.min_denominator(reliability=0.7) returns the minimum denominator for one or more reliability targets, with the count of providers above and below it.
    • Unknown names raise. An unknown .plot() option raises TypeError.

    Options at a glance¶

    Parameter Default What it does
    measure required "oe", "proportion" or "rate"
    unit, label, hover None Provider id, display name, extra tooltip columns
    facets None One funnel per value (a column or list): own center line, τ, FDR step
    exclude None A boolean column, or a list of unit ids, left out of the model
    interval 0.95 Coverage of eb_low/eb_high
    compare_to None "peers" (pooled center, spread allowed) or "model" (O/E against 1.0, no spread allowance)
    center pooled A fixed center line (a target); pair it with overdispersion=False
    overdispersion True Judge against the spread between providers (τ)
    within_dispersion 1.0 Clustered counts or survey items: a number, "estimate", or a column of per-provider φᵢ
    tau_method "spiegelhalter" "spiegelhalter", "winsorized", "ml", "cqc", "shmi"
    tau None A τ fixed in advance (a number, or one per facet)

    max_case_y, max_case_n None Per-provider SQL worst case, for p_value_drop1
    fdr_min_denominator None Volume floor for the FDR step only (independent filtering)

    benchmark None "abc" (achievable benchmark of care) or "eb" (shrunk 90th percentile), drawn as a dashed line; needs worse=
